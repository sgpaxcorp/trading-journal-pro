# Neuro Analysis Investment Portal

## Access
- Smart Tools -> Neuro Analysis.
- Public footer -> Neuro Analysis login.
- Direct route: `/neuro-analysis`.

Neuro Analysis is a private research portal for long-term company and ETF analysis. It is separate from Option Flows Analysis and separate from active trading execution. It is designed to help you document investment theses, analyze filings, track portfolio positions, and evaluate long-term dividend or compounding decisions.

## What it is
Neuro Analysis is a research workspace, not a broker and not an execution platform. You manually tell the platform what you bought, what you are watching, or what documents you want analyzed. The platform then helps you organize public data, uploaded filings, market data, thesis notes, valuation assumptions, and AI research.

The platform can support stocks and ETFs. Stocks usually have company profiles, 10-K/10-Q filings, business segments, margins, revenue history, competition, and management discussion. ETFs usually require a different lens: holdings, expense ratio, index exposure, sector allocation, dividend behavior, and concentration risk.

## What it is used for
Use Neuro Analysis to:

1. Create company and ETF profiles by ticker.
2. Upload or import filings such as 10-K and 10-Q documents.
3. Read summaries grounded in documents instead of guesses.
4. Build and revise an investment thesis with AI assistance.
5. Track whether future filings confirm or weaken the thesis.
6. Model long-term value, dividends, compounding, and shareholder reinvestment scenarios.
7. Compare companies inside a sector or market.
8. Review whether to keep waiting, add, hold, reduce, or exit.

## How to use it
Start with one ticker. Do not try to analyze the whole market first.

1. Open Neuro Analysis.
2. Enter the ticker.
3. Create or open the profile.
4. Load market data.
5. Add public filings or uploaded documents.
6. Review or approve the investment policy.
7. Write your current thesis in plain language.
8. Run the price-blind Business Quality Analysis.
9. Review the documented Management and Capital Allocation Analysis.
10. Review the multi-year Earnings Quality and Accounting Risk Analysis.
11. Review the stock valuation produced only after all three pre-valuation dossiers.
12. Review the Reverse DCF combinations and sensitivity tables.
13. Review the Independent Bear Case, its evidence, and its invalidation conditions.
14. Review the Portfolio Exposure Map and shared economic dependencies across holdings.
15. Review the Macro Context Agent and each company's documented transmission mechanisms.
16. Enter available investment capital and review the Capital Allocation Dashboard.
17. Review the deterministic decision support.
18. Create a versioned Investment Committee Packet.
19. Review every classified claim, source, and date.
20. Record the authorized human committee decision.
21. If the decision is APPROVED and an external purchase actually occurs, record its real date, price, and portfolio weight.
22. Confirm the permanent Original Investment Thesis Record created from that exact packet and decision.
23. On later research runs, review what current evidence changed relative to the original purchase-date thesis.

The best workflow is evidence first, opinion second. If the AI does not have enough data, add filings or notes before asking for a final opinion.

## Investment governance
Neuro Analysis connects the research flow into one auditable sequence:

1. Select a company or ETF.
2. Load market data.
3. Import or upload the required evidence.
4. Analyze the operating business without price or valuation data.
5. Analyze documented management actions and capital allocation without judging personality or character.
6. Analyze multi-year earnings quality and accounting relationships without making fraud determinations.
7. Calculate valuation only after all three pre-valuation dossiers are complete.
8. Reverse-engineer multiple operating combinations that approximately justify the current market value.
9. Give the working thesis and frozen evidence to an independent Bear Case Agent that cannot see a Bull Agent or final recommendation.
10. Generate the AI memorandum and compare implied expectations with company history and dated industry evidence.
11. Review the policy gate and evidence gate.
12. Create and freeze the Investment Committee Packet.
13. Record the authorized human decision against that exact packet version.
14. Register the real purchase details only after an APPROVED decision and external execution.
15. Freeze the permanent Original Investment Thesis Record.
16. Append future thesis reviews without rewriting the original baseline.

The AI can explain and challenge the research case, but it cannot approve an investment or record the final committee decision. Research never creates an approved portfolio position directly.

## Investment Committee
Every proposed investment must pass through the Investment Committee workspace before it can become eligible for a manually created portfolio position.

Each packet preserves:

1. Executive summary and investment thesis.
2. Current market price and estimated intrinsic-value range.
3. Expected-return assumptions and downside scenario.
4. Key financial metrics and balance-sheet analysis.
5. Competitive position, catalysts, principal risks, and contradictory evidence.
6. Portfolio impact and position-size proposal.
7. Conditions that would invalidate the thesis.
8. Sources and dates for every material claim.
9. The complete report, model, policy, and evidence snapshots reviewed at that time.

Every statement is labeled as **FACT**, **CALCULATION**, **ASSUMPTION**, **ESTIMATE**, or **AI INTERPRETATION**. **USER DECISION** is reserved for the authenticated person reviewing the packet. The only final committee states are **APPROVED**, **REJECTED**, **WATCHLIST**, and **NEEDS MORE RESEARCH**.

A decision is immutable. To reconsider an idea after new evidence, create a new packet version and decide that new version. APPROVED only establishes portfolio eligibility; it does not execute a trade or create a position automatically.

## Permanent Original Investment Thesis
An APPROVED committee decision does not create an investment record by itself. If the user later executes the purchase outside Neuro, the user records the actual purchase date, purchase price, and initial portfolio weight. At that moment Neuro creates one permanent Original Investment Thesis Record for the research case.

The record freezes:

1. Purchase date, purchase price, and portfolio weight.
2. Investment thesis and valuation assumptions.
3. Expected business developments and catalysts.
4. Major risks and key metrics to monitor.
5. Conditions that invalidate the thesis.
6. Supporting documents and their dates.
7. The exact Investment Committee decision and packet reviewed at purchase time.

The original record cannot be edited or replaced. New filings, notes, prices, reports, and revised narratives never overwrite it. The record is removed only as part of the user's complete account-data deletion.

Every later Neuro research run for that case compares current documented facts with the original purchase-date baseline and appends a separate review. The only thesis-status classifications are **THESIS STRENGTHENED**, **THESIS UNCHANGED**, **THESIS WEAKENED**, **THESIS INVALIDATED**, and **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**.

Each reported change shows the original expectation, the current documented fact, its effect on the thesis, and the dated underlying evidence. **THESIS INVALIDATED** requires evidence that matches an explicit invalidation condition frozen in the original record. Price movement by itself does not invalidate or strengthen the business thesis. These classifications describe thesis evidence; they are never automatic buy, sell, hold, add, reduce, sizing, or execution decisions.

## Position Exit Review
When an Original Investment Thesis exists, every later full Neuro run also creates a separate append-only Position Exit Review. Its job is to determine what changed before an existing position can be reconsidered. It does not approve, recommend, or execute an exit.

The review compares:

1. Original thesis versus current documented evidence.
2. Original valuation versus the current valuation model.
3. Original financial expectations versus actual financial results.

When supported, the review can classify the documented change as fundamental deterioration, original-thesis invalidation, material valuation change, a better documented capital-allocation opportunity, a portfolio-risk constraint, a documented liquidity requirement, a documented tax consideration, a corporate event, an original-analysis error, or another documented reason. Each reason has its own evidence gate. For example, portfolio risk requires both the current portfolio record and investment policy; liquidity and tax reasons require explicit user or policy records; thesis invalidation must match an exact frozen invalidation condition.

The only review statuses are **DOCUMENTED CHANGE REQUIRES HUMAN REVIEW**, **NO DOCUMENTED CHANGE**, and **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE**. A falling stock price alone is not thesis failure. A rising stock price alone is not thesis success. Price is shown as context, but cannot classify an exit reason without the required business, valuation, portfolio, policy, tax, or user evidence. Every saved review preserves the complete comparison used at that time and requires a human decision.

## Portfolio Exposure Map
The Portfolio Exposure Map examines economic dependencies across holdings instead of treating ticker count or sector labels as proof of diversification. Each holding is evaluated against interest rates, consumer spending, enterprise technology spending, housing, energy and commodity prices, credit conditions, government spending, healthcare reimbursement, currency, geography, major customers and suppliers, semiconductor demand, artificial-intelligence spending, and the economic cycle.

The map requires a specific dependency name and documented transmission channel. For example, two companies are not grouped merely because both are technology companies; they can be linked when evidence shows that both depend on the same hyperscaler AI infrastructure budget, the same supplier, customer, geography, currency, or economic variable.

For every verified dependency, Neuro shows:

1. Affected holdings and their current portfolio weights.
2. The gross share of portfolio value connected to the dependency.
3. Business-sensitivity magnitude and direction.
4. The documented transmission mechanism.
5. Supporting and contradictory evidence.
6. Uncertainty and missing evidence.
7. Whether the relationship crosses conventional sector labels.

The dependency graph is an evidence map, not a risk score or trading instruction. It presents exposure and magnitude for human review and never automatically recommends diversification, rebalancing, or position changes.

## Macro Context Agent
The Macro Context Agent tracks interest rates, the yield curve, inflation, employment, GDP, credit conditions, dollar strength, energy prices, and relevant commodity prices. The observed-data layer uses dated macroeconomic releases. Calculated observations, such as the 10-year minus 2-year Treasury spread or year-over-year CPI inflation, display their formula instead of being presented as raw published values.

The record keeps four evidence categories separate:

1. **OBSERVED MACROECONOMIC DATA**: published observations and transparent calculations from those observations.
2. **MARKET EXPECTATION**: dated market-implied information such as a yield curve, breakeven, or futures curve.
3. **THIRD PARTY FORECAST**: a dated forecast attributed to an external institution, industry group, economist, or consensus source.
4. **AI INTERPRETATION**: a bounded inference linked to the underlying observation, expectation, forecast, and company evidence.

For every current holding and research candidate, the agent identifies only material variables supported by dated evidence. It names the specific driver, direction, materiality, time horizon, financial line items, transmission mechanism, contradictory evidence, uncertainty, and indicators to monitor. A sector label alone is not evidence that a company has a particular macro sensitivity.

Macro context is not a market-timing engine. It does not issue BUY, SELL, HOLD, ADD, or REDUCE instructions from an economic release or forecast. Missing or unmatched sources are removed from the structured record, and the remaining context requires human review.

## Capital Allocation Dashboard
The Capital Allocation Dashboard compares the same dollar of available investment capital across existing positions, current research candidates, cash, and short-term Treasury or equivalent cash-management instruments when the approved investment universe explicitly permits that category.

For every alternative it shows:

1. Current valuation and the calculation basis.
2. Expected fundamental drivers already supported by the research record.
3. Downside scenarios and the independent Bear Case when applicable.
4. Observable liquidity data or an explicit evidence gap.
5. Economic dependencies from the Portfolio Exposure Map.
6. Current concentration and the mathematical concentration if all available capital were assigned to that one alternative.
7. Major uncertainties and evidence readiness.

The comparison unit is always the same $1. The full-capital concentration scenario is a calculation, not a proposed allocation. The dashboard does not rank alternatives, optimize a target portfolio, or execute a transaction.

**CASH is always a valid portfolio state.** Available cash is not treated as an error, residual, or instruction to invest. Treasury and cash-management alternatives do not appear merely because ETFs are allowed; the configured policy must explicitly support Treasury, T-bills, money-market, cash-equivalent, or cash-management instruments. Broad Treasury rate context is labeled as context and never represented as the verified yield or price of a specific instrument.

## Individual Capital Accounts

The Fund Business Plan workspace includes an operational Capital Accounts ledger for actual investor records. A capital pool shares one base currency and NAV per unit, while every investor keeps a separate account with initial and additional contributions, contribution dates, units issued, withdrawals, distributions, allocated expenses, current units, current NAV, historical NAV, and net investment result.

Contributions issue units and withdrawals redeem units at the latest frozen NAV per unit. Recording total pool net assets creates the next NAV close; the database calculates total units and NAV per unit and preserves the exact event cutoff included in that close. Capital events and NAV records are append-only and cannot be rewritten from the browser.

Individual return uses dated XIRR. It never divides current value by lifetime contributions when cash flows occurred on different dates. A return remains provisional when a capital movement occurred after the latest NAV or the latest NAV predates the report date. Portfolio TWR and performance attribution remain in the separate Performance Attribution Engine so deposits and withdrawals cannot appear as investment gains.

## Investment policy
The investment policy stores the user's rules before a capital proposal can be considered. It includes universe, strategy, horizon, base currency, benchmark, restrictions, liquidity needs, position limits, sector limits, cash limits, and allowed instruments.

Draft policies can be edited. An approved policy is versioned. If a required policy item is missing, Neuro can keep researching, but capital action should remain blocked.

## Decision states
Every research case should end in one of five states:

1. **INVESTIGATE**: evidence exists, but more work is needed.
2. **OBSERVE**: the profile is worth watching, but does not justify action now.
3. **PROPOSE**: evidence, policy, valuation, and quality gates support preparing a proposal.
4. **REJECT**: quality, valuation, risk, or policy makes the idea unsuitable.
5. **INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION**: required evidence is missing.

PROPOSE is not approval or trade execution. It only means the case can move to Investment Committee review with the supporting evidence preserved.

## Evidence and valuation gate
For common stocks, the strongest workflow uses recent 10-K and 10-Q filings, market data, financial history, valuation scenarios, and a thesis note. For ETFs or funds, the system uses fund strategy, holdings, fees, yield, liquidity, and market history instead of issuer 10-K/10-Q filings.

If required filings, market data, or valuation support are missing, the decision support should explain the blockers instead of pretending the case is ready.

## Business Quality Analysis Engine
For an operating company, Neuro uses a separate first pass that cannot see market price, market capitalization, valuation multiples, cost basis, position size, or price history. Valuation begins only after this business dossier is complete.

The dossier covers exactly 20 qualitative dimensions: business model, revenue sources, revenue predictability, pricing power, customer concentration, supplier concentration, competitive advantages, barriers to entry, market structure, capital intensity, return on invested capital, free-cash-flow generation, debt requirements, acquisition dependency, share dilution, management capital allocation, cyclicality, regulatory exposure, technology disruption risk, and long-term reinvestment opportunities.

There is no business-quality score. Every dimension must show:

1. The narrowest conclusion supported by current evidence.
2. Supporting evidence with source and date.
3. Contradictory evidence with source and date, or an explicit statement that none was identified.
4. Uncertainty and data limitations.
5. The additional information that would materially change the conclusion.

The dossier ends with **WHAT MUST BE TRUE FOR THIS BUSINESS TO BE AN ATTRACTIVE INVESTMENT?** Each condition includes why it matters, the evidence needed, and a failure signal. For an ETF or fund, this operating-company engine is marked not applicable and the fund framework is used instead.

## Management and Capital Allocation Analysis
For an operating company, Neuro also builds a separate documented-action dossier before valuation. It reviews acquisitions, divestitures, repurchases, share issuance, dividends, debt issuance and repayment, capital expenditures, R&D, executive compensation, insider ownership, related-party transactions, accounting-policy changes, and guidance versus subsequent results.

This module does not score management and does not infer honesty, intelligence, competence, motives, intent, trustworthiness, personality, leadership quality, or character. It reports observable decisions, financial consequences, documented inconsistencies, and unresolved questions.

When reliable same-period cash data exists, Neuro calculates how comparable incremental capital was allocated. It separates sources and uses of capital, excludes incompatible periods and currencies, and avoids double counting R&D or other operating expenses already embedded in cash flow. Balance-sheet changes alone are not treated as proof of issuance, repayment, or spending. Guidance is compared with subsequent results only when both the original statement and later outcome have verified sources and compatible definitions.

## Earnings Quality and Accounting Risk Agent
Before valuation, Neuro performs a separate multi-year accounting review. Its purpose is to identify financial relationships that require more investigation, not to accuse a company or management team of fraud and not to generate a fraud score.

The review covers net income versus operating cash flow, free cash flow versus earnings, receivables and inventory versus revenue, capital expenditures and capitalized expenses, goodwill and intangibles, acquisition accounting, stock-based compensation and dilution, one-time and non-GAAP adjustments, restructuring, deferred revenue and taxes, working capital, related parties, auditor changes, restatements, and changes in accounting estimates.

Where reliable data exists, every screen shows the raw values, periods, formula, calculated relationship, source, mitigating evidence, uncertainty, and the exact disclosure needed next. The application recomputes numeric relationships instead of trusting AI arithmetic. A threshold crossing is a research prompt that must be reconciled with business model, acquisitions, seasonality, classification, and filing notes.

All three pre-valuation dossiers are frozen with the Investment Committee Packet. The packet also freezes the independent post-valuation Bear Case. An operating-company proposal cannot be marked **APPROVED** unless Business Quality, Management and Capital Allocation, Earnings Quality and Accounting Risk, and the Independent Bear Case are ready. Other human outcomes remain available when more research is needed.

## Reverse DCF Engine
Reverse DCF starts with today's market capitalization, debt, cash, current price, and operating data. Instead of choosing one forecast and calculating a value, it estimates several combinations of revenue growth, target operating margin, reinvestment, tax rate, cost of capital, and terminal growth that approximately reconcile the current enterprise value.

The engine:

1. Produces six paired operating combinations rather than one implied scenario.
2. Shows the incremental return on capital implied by growth and reinvestment.
3. Builds revenue-growth versus operating-margin and cost-of-capital versus terminal-growth sensitivity tables.
4. Compares each implied growth, margin, reinvestment, and tax assumption with documented multi-year company history.
5. Tells the research agent which dated peer and industry evidence must be verified.
6. Ends with **WHAT MUST THIS COMPANY ACHIEVE FOR TODAY'S MARKET PRICE TO MAKE SENSE?**

The combinations are calculations, not forecasts or probabilities. A demanding combination does not automatically mean the stock should be sold, and a conservative combination does not automatically mean it should be bought. Financial institutions, REITs, ETFs, and funds require different valuation frameworks and are not forced through this operating-company FCFF model.

## Independent Bear Case Agent
After the three price-blind dossiers and Reverse DCF are available, Neuro sends only the working thesis and a frozen whitelist of evidence to a separate adversarial pass. The Bear Case Agent cannot read a Bull Agent final recommendation or the final Neuro recommendation because neither is included in its input.

It investigates competitive threats, margin compression, customer losses, debt refinancing, technology disruption, regulation, documented management execution, capital requirements, dilution, commodity and currency exposure, cyclicality, accounting concerns, valuation assumptions, industry deterioration, and alternative explanations for recent performance.

The result contains the strongest supported bear argument, dated supporting and contradictory evidence, the potential financial mechanism, indicators to monitor, and conditions that would confirm or invalidate the bear thesis. It does not assign a probability or make an investment decision. If evidence is weak, the agent must say so and list the exact information needed rather than manufacture a negative argument.

## Company profile
The company profile should answer:

1. What does the company do?
2. How does it make money?
3. What products or services matter most?
4. Who are the main competitors?
5. Is revenue growing or shrinking?
6. Are margins improving or weakening?
7. Is free cash flow consistent?
8. Does debt create risk?
9. Does management explain strategy clearly?
10. What evidence would change the thesis?

## ETF profile
The ETF profile should answer:

1. What index, strategy, or theme does the ETF follow?
2. What are the top holdings?
3. What is the expense ratio?
4. How concentrated is the fund?
5. Which sectors or countries drive returns?
6. Does it pay distributions?
7. How has it behaved across market cycles?
8. Does it overlap with positions you already own?

## Filings and documents
For stocks, filings are the core evidence layer. The most useful documents are:

**10-K**  
Annual report. Use it to understand the business, risks, financial statements, management discussion, competition, and long-term strategy.

**10-Q**  
Quarterly report. Use it to check whether the thesis is improving or deteriorating during the year.

**Annual reports, investor presentations, and earnings transcripts**  
Use these as support, but do not let marketing language replace audited financials.

## AI research agent
The AI agent should answer from evidence. It should avoid guessing when data is missing.

Good questions:

1. Summarize the business model from the latest filing.
2. What changed between the last 10-K and 10-Q?
3. What risks does management repeat?
4. What evidence supports dividend durability?
5. What would make this thesis wrong?
6. Compare this company with competitors using the uploaded filings.
7. List questions I should answer before buying more.

## Thesis builder
Your thesis should be written with clear evidence:

1. Business description.
2. Why the company or ETF deserves attention.
3. Financial evidence.
4. Competitive position.
5. Valuation assumptions.
6. Dividend or reinvestment assumptions.
7. Risks.
8. Evidence that would confirm the thesis.
9. Evidence that would break the thesis.
10. Action: watch, buy, add, hold, reduce, or avoid.

## Portfolio tracking
When you tell Neuro Analysis that you bought a position, it should track the position as part of the investment portal. The platform is not executing the trade; it is mirroring and analyzing what you report.

For each position, store:

1. Ticker.
2. Company or ETF name.
3. Purchase date.
4. Quantity.
5. Cost basis.
6. Reason for purchase.
7. Expected holding period.
8. Dividend or reinvestment preference.
9. Review date.
10. Exit or reduce criteria.

## Business plan and compounding
The business plan area projects fund value over long horizons. Monthly goals can be tracked, while compound interest should be modeled annually.

Use it to test:

1. Starting fund value.
2. Expected annual return.
3. Monthly contribution or target.
4. Annual shareholder payout.
5. Shareholders who withdraw payouts.
6. Shareholders who reinvest payouts.
7. 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30 year projections.

The projection is only a scenario. It is not a guarantee.

## Sector and market discovery
The sector screener is for finding potential opportunities. It should help compare companies within a sector by valuation, growth, margins, profitability, dividends, and evidence quality.

Use the screener to create a watchlist, not to buy automatically.

## Good research standards
Good Neuro Analysis work is:

1. Grounded in filings or reliable market data.
2. Clear about what is known and unknown.
3. Separated into facts, assumptions, and opinions.
4. Updated when new 10-K or 10-Q documents arrive.
5. Honest about downside risk.

## Important boundary
Neuro Analysis supports research and education. It does not provide personalized investment advice, does not execute trades, does not manage money for users, and does not guarantee returns.
