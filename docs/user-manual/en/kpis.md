# KPIs in plain language

## What a KPI tells you
A KPI is a measurement that helps you understand one part of your trading business. No single number tells the complete story. Read profitability, risk, activity, and discipline together, using the same date range and trading account.

## Net P&L
The total result of the selected period after adding winning and losing session results. A positive number means the recorded sessions produced a net gain; a negative number means they produced a net loss.

Compare it with the starting balance and with your Trading Business Plan checkpoint. A dollar result without that context can look larger or smaller than it really is.

## Net account change
The difference between the current account value and the reference balance. It can include trading results, deposits, and withdrawals. Use Trading P&L when you only want to evaluate execution results.

## Return on account equity
The trading result shown as a percentage of the capital used as the reference. It helps compare periods or accounts of different sizes. Confirm that the starting balance is correct before relying on this percentage.

## Win rate
The percentage of completed trades or sessions that ended positive. A high win rate is not automatically better if the average loss is much larger than the average win.

Read win rate together with average win, average loss, payoff ratio, and expectancy.

## Average win and average loss
**Average win** is the typical dollar result among profitable records. **Average loss** is the typical dollar amount lost among losing records.

This comparison helps you see whether gains are large enough to absorb normal losses. One extreme trade can distort an average, so also review the individual sessions.

## Profit factor
Profit factor compares all gross profits with all gross losses in the selected period.

- Above 1.00 means gross profits were greater than gross losses.
- At 1.00 means they were equal.
- Below 1.00 means gross losses were greater.

Use a meaningful number of trades before drawing conclusions. A very small sample can produce an impressive but unreliable number.

## Expectancy
Expectancy estimates the average result produced per completed trade or session based on the selected history. Positive expectancy means that sample produced a positive average; negative expectancy means it produced a negative average.

It describes the recorded sample. It does not guarantee the next trade or future performance.

## Payoff ratio
The relationship between the average win and the average loss. A value above 1.00 means the average win was larger than the average loss. This metric helps explain why a strategy may work with a lower win rate, or fail despite a high win rate.

## Maximum drawdown
The largest decline from a previous account high to a later low inside the selected period. It helps you understand the depth of the worst decline experienced by the recorded results.

Compare it with the maximum loss limits in your Trading Business Plan. A drawdown that breaks the plan requires a process review before increasing risk.

## Winning and losing streaks
These show the longest sequence of positive or negative results. Use them to prepare psychologically and to verify that your risk rules can survive a normal losing sequence.

## Total trades and daily activity
Total trades counts completed trading positions in the selected period. Daily activity shows entries, exits, completed trades, and the result for each day.

Review unusual increases in activity. More trades do not necessarily mean better performance and may reveal overtrading.

## Time in winning and losing trades
This compares how long profitable and losing positions normally remain open. It can reveal whether you cut winners too quickly, hold losses too long, or manage both consistently with your plan.

Compare similar instruments and trading styles. A swing trade and a scalp should not be judged by the same duration.

## Plan adherence
The percentage of reviewed sessions in which you marked that the plan was respected. Read it with checklist completion and Neuro Layer patterns.

A profitable session that broke the plan is not automatically a good business decision. A controlled loss that followed the plan can still be evidence of disciplined execution.

## When a KPI is unavailable
A KPI may be blank when the selected range has no records, the import lacks a required value, the account is not selected, or the sample is too small.

1. Confirm the active trading account.
2. Review the selected date range.
3. Complete the Journal or import the missing trades.
4. Rebuild or refresh Analytics.
5. Ask Neuro Guide which information the metric needs.
